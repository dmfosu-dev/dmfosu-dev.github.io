About

I work at the intersection of cloud-native network architecture and applied AI, building adaptive and resilient next-generation communication systems. My recent work designs control planes that anticipate load rather than react to it, provisioning SDN controller capacity from real-time packet-level features before saturation occurs.

Alongside that core technical research, I contribute to the Responsible AI Lab (RAIL) at KNUST, where my focus is AI governance and inclusivity for Ghana and other Low- and Middle-Income Countries: from digital sovereignty in government cloud procurement to scaling AI capacity across African higher education.

Research interests

  • Wireless Communications & Networking Systems
  • Next-Generation Systems (5G/6G)
  • AI/ML for Network Optimisation & Orchestration

  2. 6–10 Jul 2026

    Young Author Recognition Award at ITU Kaleidoscope 2026, Geneva

    Presented "Enforceable Digital Sovereignty in Government Cloud Procurement" at the 16th ITU Kaleidoscope Academic Conference, held alongside the AI for Good Global Summit. The paper argues that hosting data locally does not by itself deliver sovereignty: if key custody, administrative access or exit terms sit with a foreign vendor, "local" is sovereign in name only. It proposes a Sovereignty Audit Lens across five testable dimensions (locality, portability, observability, reversibility, resilience) and a seven-clause procurement schedule that turns them into enforceable contract terms. My co-author Prince Dawson Tetteh and I both received Young Author Recognition Awards.
